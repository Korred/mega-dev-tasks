Evidence that still needs investigation

The original table’s parser group includes the desk’s one-day notes. Each has its own investigation issue while remaining in the exported parser count. Their words do not prove a month/day swap or establish that all five share a cause:

  • R-41202: 12 May appears as 11 May, with a client arriving on the earlier day.
  • R-41209: every June booking is said to be one day off.
  • R-41236: the invite is said to arrive before the booking was made; no date pair is given.
  • R-41344: the same meeting shows Tuesday in Lisbon and Monday in Toronto.
  • R-41620: Tuesday 6 October appears as Monday, and the reporter says they have stopped reporting.

Check original booking input, stored date/time, timezone, invite payload and recipient display before calling these a timezone bug or another confirmed cause. Do not promise that either known fix resolves them. Keep these reports in separate investigation issues now. “Unknown” is not a shared cause. Merge investigations later only when evidence establishes the same problem, and update future accounting explicitly when evidence warrants reassignment. Waiting for a root cause before opening an issue would leave these reports hidden behind a queued parser fix.

Received is different from merged

Reports arrived from April onward. The desk did not merge any into this ticket until July; the large July merge bar includes earlier reports. That bar measures desk activity, not a sudden July onset. New reports continue into October. “Known” and “queued” do not establish that a fix shipped or that people stopped being affected. The final report also warns that silence can be under-reporting, rather than recovery.

Decision: merge only with evidence of the same cause

Read the body, look up the tenant, and inspect the channel and booking form before merging. Matching subjects can describe different problems; different subjects can describe the same problem. Intake labels are supporting clues, never the deciding key.

The explorer applies the supplied tenant-based model. Its cases show why a subject-only rule fails: the same subject can separate by channel or tenant, and different subjects can share a cause. A tenant missing from the directory has no established cause: keep the report separate and investigate. Two missing causes are not proof of a duplicate. Also keep reports separate when their bodies contradict the model or do not contain enough evidence, as the notes above illustrate.

What changes for the desk

Keep separate parser and form-hint tickets plus one investigation ticket for each unresolved report, retaining report IDs, channel, tenant and observed input/output. Ask for reproduction details where missing. Verify each fix on the affected path before closing its ticket; closing one must not close the other.

This replaces the instruction to merge when unsure. It costs more reading up front, but prevents one queued fix from hiding a different problem. Recheck this export against fresh reports before using its counts for current staffing or incident status.
